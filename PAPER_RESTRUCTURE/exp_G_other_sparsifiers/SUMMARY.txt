exp_G -- UNIFORM RANDOM SPARSIFICATION THROUGH THE PHASE-1 CONTROLS
================================================================================
Date: 2026-07-25
Raw data: results.csv (88 rows)
Forensics on the paper's broken spectral baseline: forensics.txt (separate)

Question: the Phase-1 controls were developed against DSpar. Are their verdicts
DSpar-specific, or do they hold for any sparsifier? This runs the SAME two
controls on plain UNIFORM Bernoulli edge sampling.

Setup
-----
Datasets (LCC, simple, undirected, self-loops removed):
    ca-CondMat    n =  21,363   m =    91,286
    email-Enron   n =  33,696   m =   180,811
    com-DBLP      n = 317,080   m = 1,049,866
Sparsifier: uniform Bernoulli, each edge kept independently w.p. alpha.
            alpha in {0.8, 0.9}; measured retention matched target to <0.2%.
Clustering: leidenalg ModularityVertexPartition, n_iterations=2.
Baseline:   Q_base = mean of 5 plain-Leiden seeds on the ORIGINAL graph.
Arm (a):    3 seeds per alpha. Leiden is run ON the sparsified graph; the
            resulting partition is then scored two ways --
              Q_sparse   : modularity on the SPARSIFIED graph (paper convention)
              Q_on_orig  : SAME partition, modularity on the ORIGINAL graph
            paper_delta  = Q_sparse  - Q_base
            honest_delta = Q_on_orig - Q_base
Arm (b):    degree-preserving rewire, 10*m double-edge swaps, seed 12345
            (degree sequence verified identical). A FIXED Leiden partition is
            computed once per graph (P0 on real, Prw on rewired) and held fixed
            while that graph is uniformly sparsified; 3 seeds per alpha.
            dQ_fixed = Q(P, G_sparse) - Q(P, G_full)

READING: paper_delta > 0 in 6/6 cells; honest_delta < 0 in 6/6 cells.
The sign flips in EVERY cell. Under the paper's convention uniform random edge
deletion "improves modularity" by up to +0.011; the identical partition scored
on the graph it is supposed to describe is WORSE than plain Leiden by up to
-0.025. The artifact grows as alpha falls (gap roughly doubles from 0.9 to 0.8),
which is the signature of the mechanism: modularity is measured against a null
model whose expected-edge term shrinks with m, so removing edges inflates Q for
free, independently of whether the partition got better.

Cross-check against the paper's own exp3 run: uniform_random at alpha=0.8 on
com-DBLP reported dQ_leiden = +0.0103, +0.0104, +0.0097
(PAPER_EXPERIMENTS/results/exp3_scalability/scalability_raw.csv). Our
paper_delta of +0.0078 reproduces that (same sign, same order; the small offset
is leidenalg vs igraph community_leiden and a 5-seed vs 1-seed baseline).

READING: uniform sparsification has NO Delta Q_fixed signal at all -- not at
alpha=0.8/0.9, and not at DSpar's own retention of 0.39-0.47. Every value sits
within +-0.001 of zero, i.e. inside seed noise, on the real graph AND on the
rewired null. DSpar at the same retention produces +0.044 to +0.142, two to
three ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE larger.

This is expected analytically and it is the point: under uniform Bernoulli
sampling both terms of modularity -- the within-community edge count and the
(sum k_i)^2 / (2m) null term -- scale by alpha in expectation, so E[dQ_fixed] = 0
for ANY fixed partition, on ANY graph. Uniform sampling is unbiased with respect
to the modularity null model. DSpar is not: it is degree-biased, and degree bias
is exactly what the configuration-model null isolates.

So the null arm "reproduces" uniform's dQ_fixed only in the trivial sense that
both real and rewired are zero. There is no signal for the null to explain away,
which is itself the informative result: dQ_fixed > 0 is not a generic consequence
of removing edges, it is specific to degree-biased samplers, and exp_B already
showed that for those samplers the rewired null reproduces or exceeds it
(17/17 networks). Uniform closes the loop from the other side.


================================================================================
VERDICT
================================================================================
1. Artifact I is SPARSIFIER-AGNOSTIC. Uniform random edge deletion -- a sampler
   with no structural intelligence whatsoever -- shows paper_delta > 0 in 6/6
   cells and honest_delta < 0 in 6/6 cells. The "sparsification improves
   modularity" result is a property of the SCORING CONVENTION (scoring a
   partition on the graph it was fitted to, which has fewer edges and therefore
   a weaker null term), not a property of DSpar. Any paper claim of the form
   "sparsifier X raises Q" must be restated with Q measured on the original
   graph, or it is reproduced by coin flips.

2. Artifact I and the dQ_fixed phenomenon are DIFFERENT mechanisms and must not
   be conflated. Artifact I needs only edge removal (uniform reproduces it in
   full). dQ_fixed > 0 needs DEGREE BIAS (uniform produces exactly zero at
   matched retention; DSpar produces 100-5000x more). The configuration-model
   null from exp_B attacks the second mechanism; the score-on-original control
   attacks the first. Both controls are needed and neither substitutes for the
   other.

3. Practical consequence for the rewrite: uniform random sampling is the correct
   null SPARSIFIER, and it is a demanding one. At alpha=0.8 it delivers a 20%
   edge reduction with an honest modularity cost of only -0.016 to -0.025 and
   costs essentially nothing to compute. Any proposed sparsifier must beat
   uniform on honest_delta at matched retention and matched runtime; DSpar's
   published advantage was measured under the convention that uniform also
   "wins" under, so it has not yet been shown to clear this bar.
